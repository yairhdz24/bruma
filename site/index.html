Private by design

Your voice goes where you say. Nowhere else.

Open source, so anyone can check exactly what it does.

  • No Bruma server, no telemetry, no account.
  • Audio lives in memory and is never saved.
  • Keys stay on your computer, in a file only you can read.
  • Never reads password fields.
  • Never presses Enter: it pastes and restores your clipboard.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Bruma is MIT-licensed and free. Speech-to-text runs on the provider you choose with your own key; Groq's free tier covers hours of dictation a day for most people.

Why does macOS say it can't verify the developer?

Bruma isn't notarized by Apple yet. Right-click the app and choose Open the first time, or install with Homebrew or the one-line installer, which handle it for you.

Which languages does it support?

It's tuned for Spanish and English, including mixing both in one sentence. Whisper and Nova-3 understand dozens of other languages too.

Does it work on Intel Macs or Windows?

The Mac build is for Apple Silicon today; an Intel build is on the roadmap. A Windows beta is available and uses a shortcut such as Ctrl + Space instead of fn.

How is this different from Apple's dictation?

Bruma uses modern speech models plus an editor that cleans up what you said, respects mixed languages, follows your rules and adapts to each app.
